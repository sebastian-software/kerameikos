# Terminaro

**Product:** appointment booking for small businesses: connect a calendar, define availability,
let customers book themselves.

## The idea

Every appointment is an agreement between two people. The Greeks had a picture for exactly
that: the dexiosis, the clasping of right hands, which on Attic reliefs seals treaties between
cities and bonds between people. The mark shows the result the product promises, an agreement
that stands, not the calendar that leads to it.

The name itself is Latin, not Greek: _terminus_ is the boundary stone and the god of boundary
stones, the fixed point, first in space and then in time. Its Greek relative is _terma_ (τέρμα),
the goal and turning post. The ending "-aro" is invented.

## The mark

A man and a woman as half-figures in profile, facing each other and shaking hands.

- **Navy:** hair and garments, each figure with its own garment.
- **Paper color:** faces, arms, and the clasped hands, so that the handshake in the middle is
  the first thing seen.
- **Bronze:** the two shoulder clasps, the woman's hair ribbon, and the two bracelets.

Two decisions matter and should survive any redrawing:

- **The figures touch only at the hands.** In an earlier version their garments merged at the
  bottom and the two read as one body with two heads. An agreement needs two independent
  people.
- **The man's body is broader than his arm.** A slim torso under a strong arm looked wrong and
  left the mark too light beside the others.

## What was tried and dropped

- **The earlier mark, two figures above a tiled calendar floor:** a fusion of calendar grid and
  historical figures that said neither clearly.
- **A water clock (clepsydra) on a plinth:** a prop on a pedestal.
- **A hemispherical sundial:** a round bowl in a round ring has no silhouette of its own, and
  it read as a bowl. As a metaphor it was also too small: the product is about people, not
  about measuring time.
- **Two clasped hands without bodies:** understood at once, but the motif hung in the circle
  like a bridge, with nothing to stand on.
- **Kairos, the god of the right moment, as a winged runner:** a good drawing that told the
  wrong story. It read as an angel or as Hermes, that is, as speed and delivery.

## Known limit

Two figures are each smaller than a single head. At 32 pixels the mark shows two figures joined
by a light handshake, but less clearly than the marks with one motif.

## Placement

Taken over as drawn, scaled to 96%, moved 4 units to the right and 2 units up: the man's cloak
stays clear of the paper ring, the pair stands centered, and the top of his head sits at the
frame's top limit (y = 38) like the other marks.
