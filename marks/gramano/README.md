# Gramano

**Product:** files documents automatically: a file dropped into the inbox is read, named,
tagged, moved into the archive, and made searchable.

## The idea

The name comes from the Greek _gramma_ (γράμμα), the written thing: a letter, a document. Clio,
the muse of history, is the keeper of records among the muses and is shown with a scroll. She
does what the product does: she reads what is written and keeps it.

## The mark

Clio as a half-figure in profile, head bowed, reading a half-unrolled scroll that she holds in
both hands.

- **Navy:** hair and chiton.
- **Paper color:** face, arms, hands, and the open sheet with its lines of writing in navy, so
  the sheet reads as a written page.
- **Bronze:** the two rolled ends of the scroll and the clasp on her shoulder.

## What was tried and dropped

- **A shelf of scrolls with one tagged scroll:** nine times the same element; monotonous.
- **A single scroll on its own:** a prop without anyone reading it. As with Stentaro, the
  figure using the object carried the mark, not the object.
- **The whole scroll in bronze:** the large bronze area pulled the eye away from the figure,
  and the writing on it looked ragged. Only the rolls stayed bronze; the clasp was added so
  that the figure herself carries an accent, as the figures of Terminaro do.

## Placement and tracing

Taken over as drawn, scaled to 93% so that the head keeps the same distance from the ring as in
the other marks. The two color changes against the generated drawing (sheet in paper color,
clasp as a bronze disc) are recorded in [`trace-edit.py`](trace-edit.py), which
[`tools/trace-mark.py`](../../tools/trace-mark.py) applies while tracing.
