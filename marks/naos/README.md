# Naos

**Product:** a TSX compiler that turns declarative TypeScript into lightweight Web Components,
without a framework runtime; the components ship as the `@naos-ui` package family.

## The idea

The name is the Greek _naós_ (ναός), the inner chamber of a temple, the core that the columns
and the façade are built to hold. The Naos README says the reference is loose: architecture,
proportion, and a load-bearing core rather than mythology.

What the product gives its users is building blocks: small, uniform parts that fit together
into any interface. Greek walls were built that way, from dressed stone blocks of one size,
laid in courses without mortar. The mark shows a master builder setting the next block.

## The mark

A bearded master builder as a half-figure in profile, leaning over a low wall of identical
stone blocks and setting one more block into its top course with both hands.

- **Navy:** his hair, beard, and cloak, and the blocks of the wall.
- **Paper color:** face, arms, and hands.
- **Bronze:** the block in his hands and his shoulder clasp.

Two decisions should survive any redrawing:

- **All blocks are the same size and staggered course by course.** That is the statement:
  uniform parts that fit. Without the stagger the wall reads as a bar.
- **The new block sits in line with its course,** held from both ends. Earlier versions showed
  it hovering in front of the wall, with a white hole behind it.

## What was tried and dropped

- **Two columns and a doorway to the inner chamber, from the front:** a bank portal, however
  close the crop.
- **One column built from drums, with the next drum hanging above it:** a lighthouse, and the
  same silhouette as Tereon.
- **The master builder holding a model of a temple:** a good drawing, but a finished temple
  stands for architecture as a whole; the product provides the parts.
- **A mosaic maker setting a stone into a floor:** a mosaic is seen from above, and in side view
  the floor became a wall with gaps in it. Mosaic stones are also not uniform squares.
- **The wall with the builder behind it and the block in front of it:** two viewpoints in one
  picture; the block floated. A regenerated version in a new session changed his face into a
  grim, long-bearded god and his hands into fists.

## Known limit

Between his hands little of the bronze block is visible, so the accent is among the smaller
ones in the family. Like Curano, the mark shows a man fitting a part; beard, wall, and pose keep
the two apart.

## Placement

Taken over as drawn, scaled to 92% and moved 5 units down, so that the top of his head sits at
y = 38 and three courses of the wall are visible.
