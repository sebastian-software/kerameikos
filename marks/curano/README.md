# Curano

**Product:** daily dependency care: it keeps repositories current by finding what is worth
updating and turning it into reviewed, project-aware pull requests.

## The idea

The name comes from the Italian _cura_, care. Caring for a project's dependencies means
replacing single parts in time so that the whole keeps running. The mark shows exactly that: a
craftsman fits one new spoke into a wheel that is otherwise sound.

The thought behind it is the Ship of Theseus, which the Athenians kept for centuries by
replacing its planks one at a time. The ship itself did not work as a mark (see below); the
wheel says the same thing with an object that is recognized at once.

## The mark

A wheelwright as a half-figure in profile. One hand rests on the rim of a large ancient chariot
wheel; the other holds a new spoke in front of it, pointed at the gap where a spoke is missing.
The wheel is larger than the frame shows: the window cuts it on the right and at the bottom.

- **Navy:** hair, garment, and the wheel.
- **Paper color:** face, arms, and hands.
- **Bronze:** the new spoke in his hand, the ring around the hub, and his shoulder clasp.

Three decisions should survive any redrawing:

- **The spoke is in his hand, clearly apart from the wheel, and the wheel shows the gap.** With
  the bronze spoke already in place he only held a finished wheel, and nobody could tell that
  he was doing anything.
- **The wheel is large and cut by the frame.** A wheel is understood from two thirds of it, and
  a small complete wheel left the figure with cloth showing beneath it and little room for his
  body.
- **His body runs straight down to the cut.** In an earlier version the garment narrowed at the
  waist and flared again, which made him look laced in.

## What was tried and dropped

- **An olive tree, pruned and tended:** it takes a gardener to know that cutting makes a tree
  grow, and cutting back is the wrong picture for updates, which replace and do not remove.
- **The Ship of Theseus with a shipwright, seen close:** a piece of hull read as a curved brick
  wall, and it was unclear where the man stood.
- **The whole ship on props, in strict side view:** now readable as a ship, but long and flat in
  a round frame. The man became very small, and at 32 pixels neither he nor the new plank could
  be seen.
- **A wheelwright with curly hair:** the same scene, but a third curly-haired man beside
  Stentaro and Terminaro. The wavy hair tied back sets this figure apart.
- **Bronze fittings at the ends of the spokes:** they sat on two of three spokes and took
  attention from the one new part.

## Placement

Taken over as drawn at full size. The drawing already ends body and wheel a ring's width inside
its own ring, so the frame's paper ring falls where the drawing left it. The head sits a little
higher than in the other marks (its top at y = 31), which was preferred to a smaller figure.
The paper-colored forearm is cut by the window and stays open toward the paper ring, like every
part of a mark that runs out of the frame; a closing contour along the window was tried and
looked wrong.
