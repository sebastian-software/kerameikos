# Curano

**Product:** daily dependency care: it keeps repositories current by finding what is worth
updating and turning it into reviewed, project-aware pull requests.

## The idea

The name comes from the Italian _cura_, care. Caring for a project's dependencies means
replacing single parts in time so that the whole keeps running. The mark shows exactly that: a
craftsman fits one new spoke into a wheel that is otherwise sound.

The thought behind it is the Ship of Theseus, which the Athenians kept for centuries by
replacing its planks one at a time. The ship itself did not work as a mark (see below); the
wheel says the same thing with an object that is recognized at once.

## The mark

A wheelwright as a half-figure in profile, steadying an ancient chariot wheel with one hand and
fitting a new spoke into it with the other. The wheel has three spokes; where the fourth
belongs there is a gap.

- **Navy:** hair, garment, and the wheel.
- **Paper color:** face, arms, and hands.
- **Bronze:** the new spoke in his hand. The hub ring, the fittings at the ends of two spokes,
  and his shoulder clasp are bronze as well, as metal parts.

One decision should survive any redrawing: **the spoke is in his hand, not in the wheel, and
the wheel shows the gap.** With the bronze spoke already in place he only held a finished
wheel, and nobody could tell that he was doing anything.

## What was tried and dropped

- **An olive tree, pruned and tended:** it takes a gardener to know that cutting makes a tree
  grow, and cutting back is the wrong picture for updates, which replace and do not remove.
- **The Ship of Theseus with a shipwright, seen close:** a piece of hull read as a curved brick
  wall, and it was unclear where the man stood.
- **The whole ship on props, in strict side view:** now readable as a ship, but long and flat in
  a round frame. The man became very small, and at 32 pixels neither he nor the new plank could
  be seen.
- **A wheelwright with curly hair:** the same scene, but a third curly-haired man beside
  Stentaro and Terminaro. The wavy hair tied back sets this figure apart.

## Placement

Taken over as drawn, scaled to 95% so that the shoulder and the wheel stay clear of the paper
ring.
